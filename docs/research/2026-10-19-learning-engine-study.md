# English Encyclopedia Learning Engine — research and falsifiable A1 pilot

Date: 2026-10-19. Audit baseline: main at \`2886265dee535c3873c827d4864865c079062b0e\`.
Research branch: \`experiment/a1-transfer-retention-pilot-20261019\`.
**Evidence boundary:** literature reviews support *mechanisms in some populations*, not guaranteed effect sizes for Russian-speaking A0–A1 learners or for this project. This is a scoped code audit and a pilot protocol, not an exhaustive systematic review, an independently validated CEFR audit, or an empirical outcome report.

## 1. Military/diplomatic intensity: explanatory alternatives

| Program | Who learns which language | Publicly documented regimen / assessment | Causal interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DLIFLC | Primarily US military/federal staff learn *foreign languages*. | 36–64 weeks, 7 classroom hours/day × 5 days, 2–3 hours nightly homework. Source: https://www.dliflc.edu/about/ | High total hours, instruction, selection, intensive routine and professional incentives are confounded. Not a proof of a secret teaching method. |
| DLIELC | International personnel learn *English*. | American Language Course: 6 levels/34 books, initial English Comprehension Level placement, classes at most 10, weekly 50-item multiple-choice checks with 70% threshold. Source: https://dlielc.edu/2215-2/ | Placement, instructor attention and frequent tests are plausible mechanisms. Multiple-choice score is not independently verified speaking competence. |
| FSI | US diplomats learn *foreign languages*. | 24–30 weeks = 600–750 classroom hours for easier-from-English languages; Russian in the 44-week/1100-hour group. Target is professional ILR performance for US learners; source https://2021-2025.state.gov/foreign-language-training/ | Not timelines for Russians learning English, not time-to-A1, not randomized comparative pedagogy. |
| Intensive universities | Adults with varying selection, exposure and curriculum. | Not a homogeneous intervention; compare contact hours, learner entry skill, teacher expertise, authentic need, feedback, output and outcome measurement. | No defensible single acceleration multiplier from the institution label alone. |

No public credible evidence in the reviewed sources demonstrates a secret universal ultra-fast military method. Rival explanations: (1) more hours, (2) learner selection/prior schooling, (3) small groups and teachers, (4) accountability, (5) practical stakes, (6) more comprehensible interaction, (7) feedback frequency, (8) test-target alignment, (9) extra-class exposure, (10) attrition/survivorship bias. To isolate methods, equalize total **active** time, not only calendar weeks.

## 6. Proposed Learning Engine (do NOT instantiate all this prematurely)

- **Knowledge graph:** versioned \`Skill(id, can_do, prerequisite_ids, cefr_scope)\`; \`ContentSkill(content_id, skill_id, role, editorial_version)\`. DAG validation on publish; tag each existing exercise and mission editorially.
- **Diagnostic engine:** observations distinguish closed answer-key match, teacher-rated open transfer, learner self-report, and unknown/ambiguous; no claim about latent causes without evidence.
- **Adaptive practice:** deterministic priority (due verified skill deficit > due retention > needed prerequisites > novel mission), cooldown, max cognitive load; do not automatically declare a skill mastered from 4 correct choices.
- **Mission engine:** private information-gap data and observable goal state only after the two-party requirement can actually be met. Do not call a one-person writing prompt “negotiation of meaning”.
- **Error explanation:** curated rule and reason tied to verified closed error; for free answers say *candidate* and require human review.
- **Retention engine:** immutable new prompts at day +7/+30 measured from initial, snapshot task version, no preview; report overdue separately from completed-on-time.
- **Analytics:** separate recognition / scaffolded output / unassisted output / new-context transfer / verified instructor verdict, and do not collapse into one fake “English level”.

Suggested long-term event: \`SkillObservation(user, skill, task_version, attempt_id, context_id, assistance, evidence_type, reviewer_id, observed_at, scheduled_at, verdict)\`. Restrict to minimum personally necessary data, with retention/deletion policy and parent/guardian considerations for minors. Require repeatability and rubric agreement before algorithmic personalization. Existing \`UnitProgress\`, \`MistakeReview\`, \`SpeechPracticeProgress\`, \`IndependentAttempt\` remain authoritative for their own meanings.

## 7. Scope of the actual prototype

Feature: \`/learning/transfer-pilot/a1-verb-be/\` (week 1) and \`/learning/transfer-pilot/a1-have-like-can-do/\` (week 2). **One sampled core lesson per week**, not coverage of every construct in two full weeks. Opt-in dashboard link, ordinary course unchanged. Separate first, +7d, +30d open writing situations; no exposed answer keys before response; assistance declaration; teacher example snapshotted server-side; no auto-marking. Reuses \`IndependentAttempt\` and human rubrics. Reserved \`task_variant\` 100–102; one safe partial unique constraint, no data rewriting. PRG avoids refresh re-POST; database rejects duplicate stage and stale token.

Explicit exclusions in v1: no dialog agent, no audio rerendering, no new CEFR score, no AI free-answer grading, no randomized A/B assignment, no automated teacher workload service, no push reminder or fabricated 7/30-day user result.

## 8. Pre-registered experimental protocol for later human evaluation

**Hypothesis.** Relative to the existing course with identical active study minutes, a composite of varied unaided transfer prompts and delayed re-encounters yields better **blind, human-scored performance on a third, unseen scenario at day 30**. This is not established.

1. Recruit appropriate consenting learners with baseline A0–A1, comparable exposure and device access; record prior English experience. Assign participants randomly, preferably stratified by baseline proficiency; compare *intention-to-treat*. For under-18s secure guardian permission, privacy-first retention, and an age-appropriate protocol.
2. Pretest using independent form A at day 0 before lessons. Hide prompts and answer keys from both arms. Use identical course lessons/audio/scenes and identical **active learning minutes**. Treatment uses opt-in-style novel prompts/faded help; control uses equivalent-duration ordinary practice, not inactivity.
3. Parallel, equated but not identical prompts B (post), C (+7d), D (+30d). **Do not reuse the training pilot stage prompts as outcome test prompts.** Rotate/counterbalance forms across arms and blind qualified raters to assignment, date, and assistance conditions.
4. Primary outcome preregistered: proportion of novel missions meeting semantic goal, grammar, context and true independence jointly at 30d, with rubric and at least two blind raters on an adequate subset; adjudicate discrepancies. Count unreviewed as *missing/not established*, not wrong.
5. Secondary: continuous rubric subscales (meaning/form/context), independent error self-repair on unfamiliar sentences, time-to-criterion after equal opportunity, 7d retention, novel listening without transcript, speaking human rating, and attrition. Separate aids/translation, and technical failures from inability to respond.
6. Report raw denominators, assigned N, completed N, missingness by arm, confidence intervals for between-group difference, baseline adjustment, fidelity/time-on-task, device breakdown and rater agreement (weighted kappa or ICC as appropriate). Never substitute clicks, time-on-site, or number of answers for language outcome.
7. Sample size: choose **prospectively** using a justified smallest meaningful effect, expected baseline pass rate, dropout, desired statistical power and interval precision. The current repository does not supply these parameters; do not invent N or statistical power. If feasible only as a small feasibility study, report feasibility and variance, **not confirmatory effectiveness**.
8. Stop/revise on excessive frustration, adverse accessibility/UX consequences, high missingness, unreliable rubrics or disproportionate teacher workload. Compare treatments for harms as well as gains.
9. Preserve trial version, content checksum, stage, timestamps, assigned arm, attendance minutes and scoring provenance in a privacy-reviewed system **before** running the real randomized trial; current pilot captures only some of these.
